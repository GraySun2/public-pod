# Interface design for complex games

**Research date:** 2026-10-20. **Status:** Desk research and proposed design/test methods. No new game build, participant sessions, measured usability improvement or finished UI scaffold.

**Question:** How can a game retain deep decisions while making its controls, relevant state and consequences understandable?

The recommendation is to build the future scaffold around **player decisions and truthful information**, then derive screens and controls. A component catalogue alone cannot determine what a player needs to know. Treat this as a research foundation for that later scaffold, not as a selected engine, visual theme or UI middleware package.

## Evidence boundary

Primary sources were read for this packet. Most game-specific evidence is developer explanation rather than published comparative usability data. One older productivity-software study supplies a narrow empirical comparison, not proof about games. Accessibility guidance identifies important barriers; applying it does not establish universal accessibility or intuition.

Lantern Arcade's navigation difficulty and the possibility that players did not discover its map are owner reports in the current research conversation. They motivate tests, but do not establish the cause. No current Lantern Arcade or Volans Nebula build was inspected in this pass. The Rainbreak example is authored design material from the separate Settlement Under Pressure draft, not observed player behavior.

## Proposed next decision

Review the decision/information contracts and run a small prototype study before choosing reusable runtime components. The future tool could start as a repository design-and-review kit; a live editor or engine-specific UI library needs its own requirements and scope.
