# Bounty hunter games and shows: the top 20 games and the animated space bounty hunters

Twenty bounty hunter games, nineteen of them running one loop (poster, track, confront, alive or dead, deliver, get paid), and nine animated series about bounty hunters in space. The games people remember made the capture and the chase the skill (Stranger's Wrath 88, Huntdown 85, Red Dead Redemption 2 97 on Metacritic); the ones called filler made the bounty a generated list (Starfield, Rebel Galaxy Outlaw). The shows add what the games miss: the hunter stays broke, every bounty is a person, and one rival hunter is the best villain. Written for the owner's space bounty 2.0 project on 2026-11-08, at the owner's request: "to start, I want you to research the top 20 bounty hunter videogames. Their mechanics, their story, their sales, their reviews, what they have in common, what they do differently, what they get right and what they get wrong. I also want you to look at animated films or shows that follow bounty hunters in space. Space dandy and Cowboy BEbop come to mind." The same research is a Claude Doc, [Bounty Hunter Games and Shows Research](https://claude.ai/artifact/QFSC9P3P1FvAK9AsSxbmpf), which has a drawn version of the loop.

Scores are Metacritic unless the cell says OpenCritic. Sales and player counts are publisher statements as the linked press reported them. A blank cell means no public figure was found. Outside sources are summarized, not quoted; the pages are listed in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## What they have in common

Nineteen of the twenty run the same loop; the one that didn't, Star Wars: Hunters, closed within 16 months.

1. Take the poster (boards, scans, lists).
2. Track the mark (clues, witnesses, scans). Most games skip this step and jump from poster to shootout; Red Dead, Hunt: Showdown and The Witcher 3 make it the game.
3. Confront (shootout, stealth or duel).
4. Alive or dead? Alive pays more; dead pays less or fails the job.
5. Deliver (lasso, whipcord, bag for a live one; nothing special for a body).
6. Get paid: credits buy gear and ranks, and the loop starts again.

- **Alive pays more, and taking someone alive costs something.** Red Dead Online gives 210 role XP for a live target and 90 for a dead one, and some legendary bounties fail outright if the target dies. Stranger's Wrath pays more for a bagged outlaw and leaves you open while you bag him. Star Wars: Bounty Hunter has the whipcord, Red Dead the lasso and bolas, Starfield a bribe to let the target walk. The capture tool is the hunter's signature verb.
- **The poster is the menu.** Sunset Riders (1991) and Huntdown (2020) both show the boss on a wanted poster before the stage; The Witcher 3 starts every contract at a notice board; Red Dead's hang at sheriff's offices and post offices; Elite's warrants show up on a scan.
- **Bounty money is the progression.** Stranger's rewards buy crossbow ammo and armour, Red Dead's buy role ranks and gear, Elite's buy ships, Star Wars: Bounty Hunter's unlock concept art, and Starbound's and Star Wars Galaxies' raise your rank in a hunters' guild.
- **The fight at the end of the trail is the bounty.** Huntdown, Sunset Riders, Hunt: Showdown, Starbound's gang hideouts and the Witcher's set-piece monsters all end the hunt with a boss.
- **The hunter works for the law but outside it.** Jango works for Dooku, SWTOR's hunter is branded a public enemy by the Republic, Stranger hides what he is, Silas Greaves is a drifter telling tales in a saloon, and Burton in Space Marshals is a sacked marshal.
- **Westerns under the hood, even in orbit.** Space Marshals, Wild Guns, Starbound's Peacekeepers, Rebel Galaxy Outlaw and Cad Bane (designed after Lee Van Cleef's characters) all borrow the frontier: posters, sheriffs, saloons, duels.

## What they do differently

The games split on seven things, and the first one predicts the reviews: when the hunt is the whole game it gets remembered, and when it is a side dish it gets called filler.

- **Whole game or side dish.** Huntdown, Stranger's Wrath, Space Marshals, Heat Signature and Hunt: Showdown are nothing but the hunt. In Red Dead 2, Starfield, Elite, The Witcher 3 and Starbound it is one system among many, and the side-dish versions are the ones reviewers called filler: Starfield's radiant bounties were described as inconsistent even before the exploit, and Rebel Galaxy Outlaw's reviews dwelt on the cash grind. The Witcher is the exception, because each contract was written like a main quest.
- **Where the skill lives.** Aim (Huntdown, Sunset Riders, Gunslinger), stealth and planning (Space Marshals, Heat Signature's pause button), reading the scene (Red Dead's witnesses, Hunt's three clues, the Witcher's whodunnits), flying (Elite, Rebel Galaxy Outlaw), or beating other people (Hunt, Galaxies' Jedi bounties, Red Dead Online from rank 12).
- **Who the target is.** Written characters with a story (Red Dead's 14, the Witcher's contracts, SWTOR's rivals), generated ones (Starfield, Starbound, Heat Signature, Elite), monsters (Hunt, the Witcher), or other players (Hunt, Galaxies, Red Dead Online). Written targets get remembered; generated ones get farmed.
- **Hunter or hunted.** Star Wars Outlaws and Red Dead's own wanted system flip the fantasy: the bounty is on you, hunters come for it, and the wanted level is the pressure. Trigun and Outlaw Star do the same on screen.
- **Tone.** Grim (Hunt, Weird West), pulp and comedy (Stranger's Wrath, Gunslinger's tall tales, Huntdown's 1980s action film), heroic (Metroid), tactical cool (Space Marshals).
- **Camera and screen.** First person (Gunslinger, Stranger's shooting), third (Star Wars: Bounty Hunter, Red Dead), top-down (Weird West, Heat Signature, Space Marshals), side-on 2D (Huntdown, Sunset Riders), cockpit (Elite, Rebel Galaxy Outlaw). Only Space Marshals was built for a phone first; Huntdown was ported there and Star Wars: Hunters aimed there.
- **How the money works.** A flat reward (most of them), pay that grows with the choice (Red Dead Online's time bonus and alive XP), pay that funds survival (Stranger's operation, Outlaw Star's ship upkeep), or pay that is the scoreboard (Sunset Riders: most damage on the boss takes the whole reward).

### What the shows know that the games mostly don't

- **The hunter is broke by design.** Bebop's crew misses the payout nearly every session, Dandy's hunts blow up, and Outlaw Star's ship eats every fee. Games pay out reliably, so they lose the comedy and the hunger.
- **The bounty is a person.** Every Bebop session is a target with a past, and the episodes do double duty: a self-contained hunt that also moves the crew toward the ending.
- **A rival hunter is the best villain.** The MacDougall brothers, Bane against Fennec, Spike against Vicious. The same job, the other side of it.
- **Tone comes from one borrowed genre.** Bebop takes Westerns, noir and jazz; Bane takes Lee Van Cleef; Dandy takes 1970s pulp. Each picks one and commits.
- **Being the bounty is a story too.** Trigun's Vash and Coyote Ragtime Show run the chase from the other end, as Star Wars Outlaws does in games.

## Takeaways for an original space bounty hunter game

Build the capture, the chase and the broke hunter; skip the generated target list. Ten things the research supports, then three calls it raises for the design call sheet.

1. **Make the capture tool the signature verb, with risk.** One tool (a net gun, a stun whip, a bola launcher) takes a target alive, pays more, and leaves you exposed while you use it (Stranger's Wrath, Red Dead Online). Dead still pays, but less, and a few targets fail if killed. It also gives the quick-draw and slow-mo powers from the first Space Bounty a job: they are how you survive the capture window, and they stay player-triggered.
2. **Put a Track step before every fight, even a short one.** Two or three clues (a witness, a scan, a trail) turn a spawn into a person (Red Dead, Hunt: Showdown, The Witcher 3). Most games skip it, and those are the ones called filler.
3. **Write the posters first.** A face, a name, a crime, a price, a quirk. Huntdown scored 85 with three hunters and about twenty posters; Sunset Riders is still on best-of lists 30 years on. Eight to twelve written marks beat an endless generated list (Starfield).
4. **Make delivery the climax.** The ride back is where Red Dead ambushes you and where Hunt reveals you to every other player. In space: the mark's friends chase your ship, or the mark works on the hold door. The payout gets earned twice.
5. **Keep the hunter broke.** Fuel, repairs, fines, the broker's cut: the Bebop rule. A ledger after each job where the reward shrinks is the comedy, the pacing, and the reason to take the next poster. Outlaw Star's ship upkeep is the same idea.
6. **Give the hunter one rival.** Spike has Vicious, Gene has the MacDougalls, Fennec has Bane, SWTOR's hunter has Tarro Blood. A rival racing you to the same mark is a story engine that fits a one-hour game and a jam.
7. **Sessions, not a campaign.** Bebop's 26 standalone sessions on one thin thread is the shape for phone play and for jams: one bounty per sitting, a running thread that pays off at the end.
8. **Pick one borrowed genre for the tone and commit.** Bebop took noir and jazz, Bane took the spaghetti western, Dandy took 1970s pulp. Decide it before the art picker and the sound audition pages, so both pull the same way.
9. **Scope like Huntdown or Space Marshals, not Starfield.** Small, finished, loop-first games scored 79 to 88; the big games' bounty systems were their weakest parts. Space Marshals is the proof that top-down tactical play with limited ammo and star ratings works on a phone.
10. **Three traps with names on them.** Selling the hook separately (Starfield's $7 quest), using the word without the mechanic (Star Wars: Hunters, 16 months), and a loop with no variation (Star Wars: Bounty Hunter, called repetitive in 2002 and again in 2024).

### Three calls this raises for the call sheet

Each is numbered with one pick marked; nothing is built until the owner says go.

- **The screen.** 1. Top-down tactical like Space Marshals (my pick for a phone-first jam build: proven on touch, and posters and a ledger fit a portrait screen). 2. First person like the first Space Bounty, which keeps the guns and quick-draw the owner liked ("Really nice physics and guns") and suits a PC build.
- **Who the targets are.** 1. A written roster of 8 to 12 marks, each with a clue chain and a twist (my pick: the Witcher and Red Dead lesson). 2. Marks generated from parts for replay, cheaper per bounty but the Starfield trap.
- **The money.** 1. The Bebop ledger, where costs eat the reward and the hunter stays broke (my pick: it is the tone and the pacing in one system). 2. Clean payouts that buy upgrades, as in Stranger's Wrath.
